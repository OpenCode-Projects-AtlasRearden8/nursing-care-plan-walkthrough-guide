# 03 - Smart Goals

## The idea in one sentence

A goal is a sentence about the *patient's* future behavior or status that a stranger could score as met / not met on a specific date.

The classic checklist most programs teach:

- **S**pecific — one clear behavior or measure
- **M**easurable — a number, an observable action, or a clear yes/no
- **A**chievable — realistic for this patient in this timeframe
- **R**elevant — targets the diagnosis you actually named
- **T**ime-bound — "by 1400 today," "within 24 hours," "before discharge"

## Who is the sentence about?

**Always the patient.** Goals start "Patient will…". If your sentence is about nursing ("Nurse will reposition q2h"), that is an *intervention* and it belongs in step four.

## The scoring test

Imagine a stranger reading your plan tomorrow and grading it. Can they say, honestly and confidently, "met" or "unmet"?

- "Patient will report pain is at an acceptable level" ✗ — "acceptable" is whatever they feel like that day.
- "Patient will report pain 3/10 or less with ambulation by [time] on [date]" ✓ — measurable, timed.
- "Patient will be able to walk" ✗ — unmeasurable weasel-word "able."
- "Patient will ambulate 25 feet with rolling walker and standby assist by discharge on [date]" ✓

## Parts of a complete goal

1. **Patient will** (the actor)
2. ** measurable verb** — walk, state, demonstrate, maintain, verbalize (careful: "verbalize" means *says out loud*; that's measurable)
3. **the specific behavior/status**
4. **the condition** ("with rolling walker," "on room air," "using teach-back")
5. **the deadline** (time or date)

Pick the deadline your program expects — short-term goals during a shift, long-term/discharge goals for the big picture. Many care plans want one of each priority.

## Realistic target-setting

- Base the number on your patient's actual status, not ambition. If someone stood with assist today, 25 feet by tomorrow is judicious; a mile is fiction you'll fail to evaluate.
- Tie the goal to the *problem* in the diagnosis, not to a nice-sounding generic. If your diagnosis is about knowledge, the goal must show knowledge (teach-back), not action they were already doing.

## Mini practice

Fix these:

| Weak goal | Fixed goal |
|---|---|
| Patient will drink more fluids | Patient will consume at least 1800 mL oral fluids per 24 h, by end of [date] |
| Patient will understand diabetes | Patient will correctly identify three signs of hypoglycemia via teach-back before discharge on [date] |
| Nurse will prevent pressure injuries | (That's an intervention.) Goal: Patient will maintain intact skin, no new Stage II+ injuries, by [date] |
| Patient will eat enough | Patient will consume at least 75% of each meal at two consecutive meals, within 48 h of [date] |

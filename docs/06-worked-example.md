# 06 - A Worked Example

A complete, **fictional** plan for a **fictional** patient — entirely invented case study for teaching. No real person exists in it. By the end you'll have seen all five steps in one place.

> Case: A 68-year-old admitted after a fall at home (case study). No real patient data; all names, dates, and details are invented placeholder values (marked `[date]`, `[time]`).

## Step 1 — Assessment cues (clustered)

**Mobility/fall cluster:**
- Case study states: fall at home, declined to stand by self, held onto furniture walking to chair
- Cases: unsteady during observed ambulation in hallway, 10 feet with two-person assist
- Reports "legs feel like noodles" after 20 feet of walking

**Balance/confidence cluster (subjective):**
- States "I'm scared to walk without someone holding me"
- Observed: grips bed rail while sitting on edge of bed, feet shuffling

**Vital/observation cluster:**
- Vital signs (fictional values): BP 118/72, HR 84, RR 16, SpO2 97% room air
- Lower-extremity strength 3/5 proximally per simulated exam

## Step 2 — Diagnosis (PES, three parts)

> **Impaired walking** related to musculoskeletal impairment and insufficient confidence in balance, as evidenced by unsteady gait requiring standby-to-two-person assist for ambulation, proximal lower-extremity weakness, and patient's stated fear of walking unassisted.

(Exact label and etiology wording would come from your program's approved NANDA-I reference — this shows the *pattern*.)

## Step 3 — SMART goal

> **Short-term:** By `[date]`, patient will ambulate 25 feet with wheeled walker and standby assist without loss of balance.
>
> By **discharge** (fictional scheduled `[date]`), patient will independently ambulate 100 feet with wheeled walker and demonstrate a safe fall-prevention routine back to home layout.

Check: patient-centered verbs ✓, measurable (feet, assist level) ✓, relevant to the diagnosis ✓, time-bound ✓.

## Step 4 — Interventions

1. **Assess** lower-extremity strength and gait stability; observe ambulation distance and assist level; results communicated at care-plan rounds.
2. **Coordinate** with physical therapy for supervised gait training once daily; nursing staff reinforce PT techniques between sessions.
3. **Position** patient seated on edge of bed with bed in lowest position and call light within reach **before** assisted ambulation walks.
4. **Accompany** patient for ambulation with standby assist, twice daily on nursing shifts (morning and evening), documenting distance and assist level.
5. **Teach** fall-reduction strategies (learning progression: rise-sit balance, safe footwear, lighting, path clearing); evaluate via teach-back before discharge date.
6. **Monitor** for dizziness or blood pressure changes (orthostatic precautions) with first ambulation each shift; hold activity and notify care team per protocol if symptomatic.

Each intervention targets either the **etiology** (weakness, confidence, mobility) or directly the **goal** (distance, independence).

## Step 5 — Evaluation

**(Interim, at `[time]` on `[date]`:)** Partially met: patient ambulated 18 feet with wheeled walker and standby assist (up from 10 feet with two-person assist) without loss of balance; long-term target of 25 feet not yet reached. Plan: continue current interventions, request repeat PT session tomorrow, evaluate remaining 7-foot increment at next assessment point.

**(At the goal's deadline):** would be completed at the actual time stated, using the actual measured distance — never with estimates or projections ahead of the deadline.

> Everything above is a teaching pattern from a fictional case. Your program's exact formatting (tables vs. columns, ordering, citations) may differ — follow your syllabus and instructor templates. Not medical advice; not for use with real patient data sets.

# 02 - NANDA-Style Language

## The idea in one sentence

A nursing diagnosis is a one-sentence naming of a problem you're licensed to treat, in the official-ish pattern: **problem + related to (cause) + as evidenced by (cues)**.

## The three-part structure

> **[Nursing diagnosis]** related to **[cause/factors you can act on]** as evidenced by **[your cues from step one]**

Written (PES for short):

- **P — Problem:** the NANDA-style label, e.g., "Impaired skin integrity" or "Acute pain."
- **E — Etiology ("related to…"):** the *why*. Crucially, this should be something **nursing interventions can influence**.
- **S — Signs & symptoms ("as evidenced by…"):** two to four of your clustered cues, quoted almost verbatim from step one.

The etiology is the engine of the whole plan: your goals target the problem, and your interventions target the *cause*. If the etiology is unfixable by nursing, the plan goes nowhere.

## Common mistakes (and friendlier fixes)

**1. Medical diagnoses as the etiology.**
- "Impaired gas exchange related to pneumonia" — pneumonia is a medical diagnosis; the medical team fixes pneumonia.
- Better pattern: "Impaired gas exchange related to alveolar-capillary membrane changes as evidenced by…" (use the physiological mechanism, per your NANDA reference).

**2. Unfixable etiologies ("related to death," "related to c-section").**
Pick the part nursing *can* act on — pain control, positioning, mobility, education, coping — or choose a different diagnosis that fits.

**3. Risk diagnoses with "as evidenced by."**
"Risk for infection" means it *hasn't happened yet*, so there are no signs to evidence. Use **related to risk factors** only, e.g., "Risk for infection related to invasive line and impaired mobility."

**4. Writing for the chart, not the thought.**
The label words come from the approved NANDA-I list your program gives you. The *related to* and *as evidenced by* parts are yours. Don't invent label wording; do make the rest specific.

**5. One cue propping up the whole diagnosis.**
If you only have one sign, either gather more or pick a diagnosis your evidence actually supports.

## Three flavors of diagnosis

- **Actual:** problem exists now → P + E + S.
- **Risk:** problem might develop → P + E (risk factors, no S).
- **Health-promotion:** readiness to improve → "Readiness for enhanced…" pattern.

Your program will tell you which flavors are required.

## Mini practice

Which etiology is written like a nurse?

| Diagnosis stem | Etiology | Nurse-like? |
|---|---|---|
| Impaired physical mobility | related to hip fracture | No — medical diagnosis; use "related to musculoskeletal impairment" (per reference wording) |
| Ineffective airway clearance | related to retained secretions | Yes — nursing can act on secretion clearance |
| Risk for falls | related to unsteady gait, sedating medication effects | Yes — risk factors nursing monitors and mitigates |
| Acute pain | related to appendicitis | No — use the mechanism ("related to injury agents / surgical revision" per your reference) |

Confirm exact label and etiology wording against **your program's approved NANDA-I reference** — the pattern is taught here; the sanctioned wording lives there.

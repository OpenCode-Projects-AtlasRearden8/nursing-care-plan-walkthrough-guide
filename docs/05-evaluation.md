# 05 - Evaluation

## The idea in one sentence

Evaluation is scoring the goal — `met`, `partially met`, or `unmet` — at the time you wrote, using the *same measure* the goal named, and then deciding what's next.

## The honest three

- **Met** — the measurable target hit, by the time named. Cite the supporting data. Plan next steps: continue monitoring? Discharge goal?
- **Partially met** — progress, target not fully reached. State what changed and what remains. The plan doesn't die; it gets revised: new cue data, tweaked interventions, adjusted timeframe.
- **Unmet** — no meaningful progress. Not shame, information. Your evaluation becomes new *assessment* data → new or refined diagnosis → new goal → new interventions. The loop.

## How to write an evaluation

Pattern: **[status] + [the evidence] + [the decision/next step]**

- "Met: patient ambulated 30 feet with rolling walker and standby assist at 1030 on [date]; goal met. Plan: progress to hallway ambulation BID, reassess tolerance."
- "Partially met: patient consumed 60% of meals at lunch but 25% at dinner on [date]; small frequent snacks added; dietitian consult requested; reassess at 48 h."
- "Unmet: patient reports pain 7/10 with ambulation at [time] despite interventions on [date]; reassess pain regimen with care team; revise plan."

## The photographs-vs-blueprint trap

It's easy to write an evaluation before the deadline by vibes. Don't. Evaluate *at* the time specified, with *the measure the goal specified*. If the deadline hasn't happened, you have interim assessment data, not an evaluation yet.

If the goal turns out to be the wrong goal (too easy, too hard, wrong target), *say that* and revise. Plans are living documents; "I kept the plan and adjusted for new data" reads as competence, not waffling.

**Note on honesty:** "Met" that nobody documented is a paper fiction; before writing `met`, confirm the supporting evidence actually exists in the record. If the data isn't in the chart, the goal isn't evaluated yet — go collect it.

## The full loop, again

```
reassess ──► re-diagnose (if needed) ──► rewrite goal ──► revise interventions
                            ▲                                   │
                            └───────── evaluate ◄───────────────┘
```

The care plan is never "done" until the patient's off your service — every evaluation feeds the next assessment.

Odd, human advice: **be generous to your future self.** Write evaluations in the same neutral, specific voice as your cues. You (or your preceptor, or a covered patient's next caregiver) will read it and need to *act* on it.

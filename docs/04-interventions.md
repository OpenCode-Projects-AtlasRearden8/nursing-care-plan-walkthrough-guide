# 04 - Interventions

## The idea in one sentence

Interventions are the *nursing actions* — performed by nursing staff and documented for the chart — each one tied to either the cause (etiology) from your diagnosis or the goal you just wrote.

## Two broad categories

- **Independent (nurse-initiated):** positioning, mobility, education, monitoring, therapeutic communication — things RNs do on their own judgment.
- **Collaborative / dependent:** medicine administration, wound care per protocols, anything ordered — nursing *implements*, but the action flows from an order or protocol.

Both belong in a plan. Programs usually want a mix, clearly marked per your school's conventions.

## How to write one that grades well

Format most instructors like:

> **[Verb in future/imperative] + [observable action] + [frequency/amount] + [method or purpose]**

- "Reposition patient every 2 hours while in bed, per turning schedule" — verb, action, frequency, reference.
- "Teach patient three safe transfer positions; evaluate by teach-back before discharge" — action + how you'll verify it.
- "Assess pain intensity (0–10 scale) 30 minutes after each analgesic dose" — timing matters.

## Worse, better, best

| Version | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Monitor patient | Too vague — monitor *what*, how often, for what? |
| Monitor vital signs every 4 hours and PRN; report temperature >38.0 °C to provider | Watchable, actionable |
| Assess vital signs q4h and PRN, trend SpO2 with activity, report sustained SpO2 <92% to provider to guide oxygen therapy decisions | Single action, frequency, threshold, purpose |

## The two-column secret

Instructors often check **alignment**: does each intervention trace to the etiology or the goal?

- Diagnosis: *Ineffective airway clearance related to retained secretions as evidenced by…*
- Goal: patient expectorates secretions effectively by [date].
- Interventions pick: aid cough/airway clearance techniques, hydration, positioning, assess breath sounds and secretion burden.

If you can't draw a line from a goal or the etiology to an intervention, ask: is this action doing a job here, or is it padding? Padding gets crossed out.

## Don't forget

- **Documented** — an unfound intervention never happened.
- **Priority ordering** — airway, breathing, circulation, safety basics first, then the rest, per program's priority scheme (usually ABCs / Maslow).
- **Include the "watch for" items** — the safety nets: what you're monitoring for, what triggers escalation.
